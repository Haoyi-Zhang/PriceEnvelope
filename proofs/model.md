# Mathematical interface

The complete arguments are in `theory.pdf`, included in this repository. The labels below refer to that manuscript; they are not executable proof objects.

For row s, let f_s(z)=v_s/(a_s+b_s^T z), v_s>0, a_s>||b_s||_1, and z in [-1,1]^d. Set M_1=max_z sum_s f_s(z), R=sum_s v_s/(a_s-||b_s||_1), and M_r=max_(z^1,...,z^r) sum_s max_j f_s(z^j). Maxima occur at corners by convexity. All stamps refer to the same affine family. A temporal delay bound alone does not establish this family or permit arbitrary time-varying coefficients to be treated as fixed.

For support at most k and relative radius at most theta<1, the manuscript's `thm:generalK` gives the smallest uniform constant K with R<=K M_r by a finite column-distribution polynomial. Its general optimization is not implemented as a scalable solver. For support two the five explicit factors are 2(1+theta)/(2-theta^2), 2/(2-theta), 3/(3-theta), 6/(6-theta), and 8/(8-theta), for one through five stamps. The first two are finitely attained; the latter three are limiting suprema over growing finite families. The five-stamp evaluation uses the split graph `L(K_5)` and the exact finite complete-pair value is `P_5(2)=4`, `P_5(d)=floor(7d^2/4-d)` for `d>=3`. Code evaluates rational identities and bounded examples.

The sign-conflict graph joins two rows with opposite nonzero coefficients in some latent coordinate. The exact stamp threshold for M_r=R is its chromatic number. Thus the fixed-r equality decision is polynomial for r=1,2 and NP-complete for every fixed r>=3 in the unrestricted-support class; the reduction realizes arbitrary conflict graphs with relative radius at most 1/2. Weighted deletion yields quantitative deficit bounds. These graph constructions are not new coloring algorithms. The complete pair family links saturation to classical binary covering arrays; the established covering-array extremal facts are attributed in the manuscript.

The hardness reduction gives M_1=15|E|+MaxCut(G) with two-coordinate rows 30/(4 +/- (z_u-z_v)/2). This gives NP-complete strict unsafety and coNP-complete safety. The separate fixed-stamp coloring hardness uses unrestricted row support; it is not claimed as support-two coloring hardness.

For a supplied rooted tree decomposition, each factor has one owner. A table U_t on the parent separator certifies every bag assignment by U_t>=local_factors+sum_child U_child. Positivity, running intersection, complete table coverage, and ownership are essential. Induction over the tree proves that the root bounds M_1. An externally supplied capacity must be at least that root. A solver output alone is not a certificate.

The four-coordinate worked model has M_1=9/2 and R=5. Its leaf table is (3/2,2), middle table (3,14/5), and root 9/2. A maximizing corner is (-1,-1,+1,-1). All of these are retained in exact rational encodings.

Common attenuation gamma=min(1,min_l c_l/M_l) ensures target feasibility when each M_l bounds all admissible proposals. Between a feasible old command and a feasible target, apply every coordinate decrease to min(old,target), acknowledge actual application, then apply increases. Nonnegative routing makes every intermediate vector coordinatewise below one of the two feasible endpoints. Epoch/phase enforcement and serialization are assumptions of this mathematical command interface; queue occupancy, packet propagation, lost acknowledgments, and failures are not modeled.

The virtual dual contraction is a separate sufficient result. It requires a global optimum inside a positive-price box, weighted diagonal dominance, a bounded read age D, bounded update gap B, and controlled additive errors. Constant error yields a residual radius, not exact optimality. Vanishing errors and shrinking certified price descriptions give convergence under the stated assumptions. The finite evidence has two distinct control cases. A three-stamp complete support-two family satisfies `M_3=21<R=24` at capacity 21; correlation-aware attenuation is one, rowwise attenuation is `7/8`, every two-phase subset state is safe, and unbarriered endpoint mixing has load 24. A separate sign-compatible two-link case has `M_1=M_r=R` and tests delayed contraction. Neither uses a trained predictor or establishes packet-level acceleration.

A negative control illustrates the clipping distinction: q=(2+z_1,2+z_2,2-(z_1+z_2)/2), weights=(1,1,4). Coherent M_1=14/3. At raw z=(2,0), independently clipping path prices to [1,3] gives (3,2,1), load 29/6>19/4. Clipping the shared latent point first gives z=(1,0) and load 7/2. Independent interval membership does not recover the lost common-latent constraint.
